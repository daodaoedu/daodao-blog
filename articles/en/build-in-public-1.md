If you've known Dao Dao for a while, you may remember that we've been exploring how to help people learn independently, sustain their practice, and document their learning journeys.

Our product was originally designed primarily around the needs of individual learners.

But through organising learning challenges and designing different learning experiences, we began to notice something. When people have someone who notices their efforts, responds to their experiences, or learns alongside them, they are often more willing to keep trying and discover new perspectives through shared experiences.

This led us to reconsider our approach. Beyond supporting individual learners directly, could we also support the people who help others grow?

We're gradually evolving Dao Dao from an individual learning tool into a platform for coaches, mentors, trainers, consultants, and facilitators to manage learning programmes, understand participants' experiences, and provide meaningful feedback and support.

We haven't lost our focus on learners. By supporting learning professionals, we hope to create better learning experiences for more people.

## What happens after a good learning experience?

Imagine a career development session, a communication workshop, or a four-week guided practice programme.

During the experience, participants discover new ideas and may feel motivated to put them into action. But once they return to their everyday lives, things become more complicated.

Some struggle to get started. Others try something and encounter difficulties. Some make progress without even noticing it.

For coaches, mentors, consultants, and others who guide learning experiences, understanding what happens during this period isn't easy.

Even with feedback surveys, assignments, or subsequent meetings, meaningful details can go unnoticed.

Over the past month, we've been exploring how Dao Dao could help address these challenges.

## 01｜Making everyday practice easier to document

Imagine a participant who is learning to give more effective feedback to their colleagues.

Today, they try a different approach during a workplace conversation, observe how their colleague responds, and consider what they might do differently next time.

At Dao Dao, we call this short record of practice a **check-in**.

A check-in is more than marking a task as completed. It gives participants an opportunity to reflect on their experiences and discoveries, while helping the people guiding their learning understand what happens as participants put their learning into practice.

But we've been asking ourselves how to make this kind of reflection easier to sustain.

Forgetting to record an experience, not knowing what to write, or having to switch between different tools can introduce enough friction to make a meaningful activity difficult to maintain.

Recently, we've been exploring several questions:

- How can participants access their practice records more easily?
- Could one shared link take each participant directly to their own check-in space?
- How can reflection fit into people's existing daily habits?
- How much guidance is helpful before reflection starts feeling like another assignment?

We want a check-in to be more than another item on a to-do list. It should offer a brief moment to pause, make sense of an experience, and consider what to try next.

## 02｜Helping participants recognise their own progress

Imagine spending a month practising something, only to receive a summary of your check-in count and completion rate.

One participant might complete twenty practices without knowing what they've learned. Another might complete only eight but discover an approach that genuinely works for them.

This has led us to rethink how we represent the outcomes of a practice.

At the beginning, we want participants to consider a simple question:

**What would I like to get out of this practice?**

At the end, they could look back and consider:

- What have I discovered?
- How did the experience differ from my original expectations?
- What would I like to continue exploring?

We're also exploring how to connect scattered practice records into a meaningful learning journey, helping participants revisit their attempts and discoveries.

Of course, not every workshop or short programme will immediately lead to noticeable behaviour change. Sometimes, a new perspective, a deeper understanding of oneself, or a willingness to try a different approach is a meaningful outcome in itself.

We want Dao Dao to help make these less visible changes easier to recognise.

## 03｜Turning everyday reflections into meaningful feedback and support

When a programme involves ten, twenty, or more participants, reading everyone's individual reflections can become difficult.

Yet these everyday records may contain valuable information that helps the people guiding participants decide what to do next.

For example:

- Who has been making repeated attempts but encountering similar obstacles?
- Are several participants struggling with the same problem?
- Which experiences might be worth discussing in the next session?
- Has someone discovered an interesting approach that others could learn from?

We're exploring how to make these insights easier to discover while reducing the administrative work involved in organising information.

Recently, we've also begun experimenting with connecting AI to Dao Dao's programme management capabilities. This is still at an early technical validation stage.

For us, the value of AI isn't simply its ability to generate a summary. It's its ability to help people identify what deserves greater understanding and attention.

Understanding someone's circumstances, offering meaningful feedback, and recognising when additional support is needed still require human expertise and judgement.

## 04｜Why would people need another tool?

This is a question we regularly ask ourselves.

If people already use messaging groups, online course platforms, Google Forms, and other familiar tools, why would they need Dao Dao?

We don't believe every learning experience needs to move to a new platform.

But when we want to understand how participants apply what they've learned in their everyday lives, how their experiences accumulate through repeated attempts, and how those experiences can inform subsequent conversations and feedback, there are still gaps between existing tools that we want to explore.

The experience Dao Dao needs to make better is the connection between learning, practice, reflection, and feedback.

And we need to demonstrate that this experience provides enough value to justify introducing another tool, without creating another piece of administrative work.

## What's next?

Whether you provide career consultations, design guided practice programmes, facilitate workshops, or lead 30-day group challenges, we want Dao Dao to support your work by helping you stay connected with participants, understand their everyday practice, and recognise the changes that emerge over time.

Previously, we asked ourselves how we could help an individual learn better.

Today, we're also asking how we can give the people who help others grow better tools and environments to do what they do best.

Through Building in Public, we'll continue sharing our observations, product decisions, trade-offs, and questions that don't yet have answers.

If you've ever finished delivering a learning experience and wondered what happened to the participants afterwards, we'd love to hear your story.
